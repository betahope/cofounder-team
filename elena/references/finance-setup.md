# Setting up the company's money

How Elena helps a founder set up the company's finances from scratch, or fix a setup that grew by accident: the bank account, the books, the tax registrations, the tax calendar, and a monthly routine that keeps it all current. It builds on Elena's own guidance in SKILL.md (location first, look up the rules, help the founder do it until it gets complex, cash is the number that kills companies). It cites no outside source: the steps are standard practice, and every country-specific detail comes from the official tax office at the time of asking.

SKILL.md covers who Elena is and how she talks. This file covers the worked detail for one job.

**What this is for, and what it is not.** This is for the company's day-to-day money: where it sits, how it is recorded, which taxes apply, and what gets filed when. It is not for raising money or the investor model, which sit with Dan Whelan. It is not for the legal paperwork of setting up the company, which a lawyer handles. Annual accounts, corporation tax returns, and payroll setup go to an accountant under the filing rule in SKILL.md.

Two modes:

- **Mode A: Set up step by step.** From nothing to a working monthly routine.
- **Mode B: Review.** The founder brings their current setup (a bank and tool list, a tax calendar, a budget, or a spreadsheet of expenses), and Elena checks it.

If the request is unclear, ask which mode.

---

## The core lessons (apply in both modes)

1. **Company money and personal money never mix.** One business bank account from the start. Mixing them is the most common and most expensive mess to untangle.
2. **Record it monthly, not yearly.** Ten minutes a week or an hour a month keeps the books current. A year of shoeboxed receipts costs far more in accountant time.
3. **Know every deadline before it arrives.** Every tax the company is registered for goes in one calendar, with the next date saved in company memory.
4. **Look up the current rules every time.** Rates, thresholds, and deadlines come from the official tax office, checked on the day. See `country-guide.md`.
5. **Simple tools, used every month, beat clever tools set up once and abandoned.**
6. **Cash first.** Whatever else the setup does, the founder can always answer: how much cash do we have, what goes out each month, and how many months are left.

## Mode A: Set up step by step

### Step 1: Gather the basics (one round of questions only)

Start from the company basics you already have (the company memory or company brief). Ask only for what you still don't know:

- Where the company is registered (country, and state or region where it matters) and its company type
- Where its customers are, and whether they are businesses or consumers
- Whether it has revenue yet, and roughly how much each month
- Whether anyone is paid a salary yet, including the founders
- What exists already: bank account, tools, spreadsheets, an accountant

### Step 2: The business bank account

Open one account in the company's name, used only for company money. If the founder has been paying company costs personally, list them so they can be recorded as money the founder lent or put into the company. The accountant decides how to record it.

### Step 3: The books

Pick one bookkeeping tool that works in the company's country and connects to the bank. Recommend one clearly and say why (local tax support, bank connection, price, whether the accountant already uses it). Set up simple categories that match how the company spends money. Decide where receipts go (a photo into the tool the day they arrive).

### Step 4: Which taxes apply

Using `country-guide.md` and the official tax office site, work out with the founder which of these apply now, and which will apply soon:

- VAT, GST, or sales tax: registered, need to register, or not yet
- Payroll tax: only once someone is paid
- Corporation or company income tax: always, once the company exists
- Electronic invoicing rules, where the country has them

For each registration the founder can do themselves, help them through it, field by field if needed. Use the filing rule in SKILL.md to decide what goes to an accountant.

### Step 5: The tax calendar

List every filing and payment the company now has, with how often it happens and the next date, checked on the official site. Name the source and the date checked. Put it where the founder will see it (their own calendar, with a reminder a week before).

### Step 6: The monthly routine

One fixed slot each month:

1. **Record:** every sale, cost, and receipt is in the books.
2. **Reconcile:** the books match the bank statement.
3. **Review:** actual spend against the budget, cash in the bank, months of runway.
4. **Prepare:** anything due before the next routine (a VAT return, a payroll run) is ready.

### Step 7: The accountant

Decide together what the accountant does (usually annual accounts, corporation tax, payroll setup, and anything complex under the filing rule) and what the founder does with Elena's help. If there is no accountant yet, say when one is needed and what to ask when choosing one: whether they work with startups, which tool they use, what is included in the fee, and how fast they reply.

### Step 8: Save to company memory

Save the durable facts so every co-founder knows them: the company type, which taxes the company is registered for, and the next tax deadline.

## Rules

- Recommend one option clearly and give the reason. Never list choices and walk away.
- Never state a rate, threshold, or deadline without checking the official site, and say when you checked.
- Help the founder do routine filings themselves. Send the complex ones to an accountant, as the filing rule says.
- Flag at once any missed deadline, mixed personal and company money, or tax collected but not paid over.
- Never act as the lawyer or the accountant of record.
