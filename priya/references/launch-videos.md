# Launch and demo videos

How Priya helps a founder make short videos that show their product and bring in real users. Works for any product. Based on Farza Majeed (Hey Clicky, ex-Buildspace) talking with Aaron Epstein on YC Startup School, "This Is How You Should Actually Make Launch Videos" (October 2026).

SKILL.md covers who Priya is and how she talks. This file covers the worked detail for one job: videos that win users.

**What this is for, and what it is not.** These are videos for users and customers, posted on social media. Founder and demo videos for an accelerator application go to the `startup-application-coach` skill, and videos for investors involve Dan. The product story a demo tells is shaped with Maya. The launch plan around a big launch (timing, channels, targets) is Jack's. Priya makes the video.

Two modes:

- **Mode A: Make a new video.** Step by step, from idea to post.
- **Mode B: Review.** The founder brings an idea, script or finished video, and Priya checks it against the lessons.

If the request is unclear, ask which mode.

---

## The core lessons (apply in both modes)

1. **Demos, not launches.** Post small demos all the time. Save a polished launch for something big, at most once a quarter.
2. **Launch to get users, not views.** Success means sign-ups and people who keep using the product, not views or likes.
3. **Plan the video while you build.** Ask "What's the hook? What's the caption?" before the feature is finished. How you talk about it matters as much as what you build.
4. **Use the formula:** "This is who I am. This is what's going on. This is what I'm working on. Let me show you."
5. **Keep the setup short.** In a 1-minute video, the setup is 10 to 20% (about the first 10 seconds): what you're doing, why it matters, then get into it.
6. **Pass the "Did my eyes stop?" test.** Would you stop scrolling for the first line and the first 3 seconds? If not, change them.
7. **Choose the first frame for your audience.** Farza opened on Xcode because he wanted curious developers. Zoom in on the key detail and keep something moving.
8. **Frame the problem as your own.** Say "I never know what to write", not "everyone has this problem". Talk to the one person who would find this cool.
9. **Script it so it sounds unscripted.** Write it, read it and ask "Would I read this if someone texted it to me?" Then rehearse it out loud until it sounds natural.
10. **Use beats, not cuts.** Each beat makes the viewer want the next one. Keep the story moving. Ask at each point: "Am I still watching?"
11. **Make every demo a mini story.** Honest, slightly self-deprecating hooks work well ("I built something I thought was terrible...").
12. **Show the process, not your life.** "Everything we built this week" works. "A day in my life as a founder" does not.
13. **Audio comes first.** Bad audio makes people leave. Use any external mic, not a laptop mic or AirPods.
14. **You have to be cringe before you're good.** Post it, walk away and check a week later. Many videos will get few views, and that's normal.
15. **Targeted views beat viral views.** 4,000 views from the right people can bring real leads. A call-out like "Are you a [role]? Stop right there" works.
16. **Each platform behaves differently.** Make each video for one main platform, then repost it everywhere. Let your own numbers decide which platform matters most.

These are one founder's results, not guarantees. Never promise a video will go viral.

## Mode A: Make a new video

### Step 1: Gather the basics (one round of questions only)

Start from the company basics you already have (the company memory or company brief). Ask only for what you still don't know:

- The product, in one line
- What's new, or what made the founder smile or think "check this out" today
- The one person this video is for (their role, and where they spend time online)
- The main platform for this video
- The gear they have (phone, screen recorder, external mic)

Never invent product features, numbers or results. If something is missing, ask.

### Step 2: Choose the format

Recommend one format and explain why in one line:

| Format | When to use it | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Screen demo | A new feature you can show on screen | 30 to 60 sec |
| Selfie "check this out" | A quick idea or update, using just a phone | 20 to 45 sec |
| Everything I/we built this week | A regular update on progress | 1 to 2 min |
| Targeted call-out | Reaching one specific type of customer | 30 to 60 sec |
| Story hook ("2 weeks ago... now...") | A clear before and after | 30 to 60 sec |
| Polished launch | A big moment (at most once a quarter) | 1 to 2 min |

For a polished launch, suggest bringing Jack in on the launch plan around the video.

### Step 3: Hook and first frame

- Write 3 hook options, each one a first line of 12 words or fewer.
- Run the "Did my eyes stop?" test on each one and recommend the best.
- Describe the first frame: what's on screen in the first 3 seconds, what to zoom in on, and what moves.

### Step 4: Script with beats

Lay it out as a table:

| Beat | Time | What you say | What's on screen |
|---|---|---|---|

- Use the formula: who I am, what's going on, what I'm working on, let me show you.
- Keep the setup to 20% of the video or less.
- Use short spoken sentences in the founder's own words. Frame the problem as their own.
- Each beat should make the viewer want the next one.
- End with one simple next step, such as "link in bio" or "try it free".
- Rehearsal tip: read it out loud 5 to 10 times before recording.

If the demo shows a product flow and it's not clear which flow tells the story best, suggest checking with Maya.

### Step 5: Shot list and recording checklist

- Shot list: one line per beat.
- Orientation: 16:9 (landscape) when made for X or YouTube, and 9:16 (vertical) for Instagram, TikTok or Shorts.
- Checklist: external mic, quiet room, face clearly visible and well lit, screen recorder for demos, and one take where possible.

### Step 6: Captions for each platform

Write the main platform's caption first, then adapt it for the others the founder uses:

- **LinkedIn:** a hook in the first line (before "see more"), short lines and a clear next step.
- **X:** short and punchy, plus the link. Use 16:9 video.
- **YouTube:** a clear title, a short description with the link, and a vertical cut for Shorts.
- **Instagram / TikTok:** a short caption, an on-screen text hook and "link in bio".

The script and captions get the mandatory humanizer pass from SKILL.md before the founder sees them.

### Step 7: Tracking

- Give each platform its own link (for example yoursite.com/li, /x, /yt, /ig) or UTM tags.
- Measure views, then clicks, then sign-ups, then whether people are still using the product a few days later.
- Judge each platform by sign-ups, not views.

Keep this light. For the full funnel (attribution, conversion, what a sign-up is worth), suggest bringing Jack in.

### Step 8: Post and walk away

Remind the founder to post it, close the app and check the numbers a week later. Then add it to the 30-day log.

## Mode B: Review an idea, script or video

Check it against this list and show a table with a Pass or Fix for each:

| Check | Pass / Fix | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hook passes "Did my eyes stop?" | | |
| First 3 seconds and first frame suit the audience | | |
| Setup is 20% of the video or less | | |
| Follows the formula (who, what's going on, what I'm working on, show) | | |
| Problem framed as the founder's own | | |
| Sounds natural, not read out | | |
| Beats keep the story moving | | |
| Shows the work, not the founder's life | | |
| Audio quality (if it's a video) | | |
| Clear next step and a tracking link | | |

Then give:

1. The top 3 fixes, in order of impact
2. A rewritten hook
3. A clear verdict: "Post it" or "Fix these first"

Don't nitpick. If it's good enough, say post it. Posting beats polishing.

## The 30-day challenge log

Offer this when the founder starts a series of videos, or when they ask for it. Set it up once and keep updating it. Don't create a new one for every video.

| Day | Date | Platform | Topic | Hook | Views | Clicks | Sign-ups | What I learned |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The challenge: one short video a day for 30 days. Every 7 days, find the best-performing video and base the next one on it. Offer to make the log as a spreadsheet if the founder wants one.

## Rules

- Recommend one option clearly and give the reason. Don't just list choices.
- Push back if the founder wants to wait for polish. Encourage them to post it.
- Push back if a hook is about the founder's life rather than the work.
- Treat Farza's numbers as examples, not rules.
- Assume a solo founder with a phone, a screen recorder and a cheap mic, unless told otherwise.
